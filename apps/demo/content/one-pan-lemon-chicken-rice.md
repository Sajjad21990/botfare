# One-pan lemon chicken and rice

Bone-in chicken thighs and long-grain rice take about the same time in the oven, so they can share a pan. Browning the thighs first renders their fat and leaves brown bits on the bottom of the pan. The rice then toasts in that fat and soaks up the stock, the lemon and the juices that drip from the chicken. You need a wide pan that can go on the hob and into the oven, about 30 cm (12 in) across, with a lid or a sheet of foil.

## Ingredients

- 8 bone-in, skin-on chicken thighs (about 1.2 kg / 2½ lb)
- 1½ tsp fine salt, plus a pinch for the onion
- ½ tsp ground black pepper
- 1 tsp dried oregano
- 2 lemons
- 1 tbsp olive oil
- 1 medium onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, finely chopped
- 300 g (1½ cups) long-grain white rice
- 600 ml (2½ cups) hot chicken stock
- A handful of chopped parsley or dill

## Method

1. Pat the chicken dry and season it all over with the salt, pepper, oregano and the finely grated zest of 1 lemon. If you have time, leave it uncovered in the fridge for anywhere from 30 minutes to a day. The salt seasons the meat more deeply, and dry skin browns better.
2. Heat the oven to 200°C (400°F). Rinse the rice in a sieve until the water runs mostly clear. Rinsing removes loose surface starch, so the grains cook up separate instead of gluey.
3. Heat the oil in the pan over medium heat. Lay the thighs in skin-side down, in two batches if they do not fit in one layer, and cook without moving them for 8 to 10 minutes, until the skin is deep golden and much of the fat has melted out. Turn them for 1 minute, then lift them onto a plate. They finish cooking in the oven.
4. Pour off all but about 2 tbsp of fat. Add the onion with a pinch of salt and cook for 5 minutes, scraping up the brown bits as the onion releases its water. Add the garlic and cook for 1 minute.
5. Stir in the rice and cook for 2 minutes, until every grain is coated in fat and the edges look translucent. Coated grains absorb the liquid evenly and stay separate.
6. Pour in the stock and the juice of the zested lemon, about 3 tbsp, and bring to a simmer. Taste the liquid. It should taste well seasoned, because this is your only chance to season the rice.
7. Nestle the thighs into the rice skin-side up, keeping the skin above the liquid so it stays crisp. Cut the second lemon into thin slices and tuck 4 or 5 of them between the thighs. Cover with the lid or foil and bake for 25 minutes. The cover holds in the steam that cooks the rice.
8. Uncover and bake for 15 minutes more, until the rice is tender, the liquid has been absorbed and the skin has crisped again. The chicken is safe at 74°C (165°F) in the thickest part, with the probe not touching the bone, but thighs are more tender at 80 to 85°C (175 to 185°F), where their connective tissue has softened.
9. Lay a clean tea towel over the pan and leave it for 10 minutes. The rice finishes steaming and firms up, and the chicken juices settle. Scatter with parsley or dill and serve with the rest of the lemon cut into wedges.

## Variations

If your pan is smaller, use 6 thighs and keep the rice and stock the same. A couple of handfuls of frozen peas, scattered over the rice when you uncover the pan, add colour and need no thawing. Brown rice needs about 900 ml (3¾ cups) of stock and close to an hour under the cover, so it does not suit this recipe's timing.

## Leftovers

Cool leftovers quickly, refrigerate them within an hour and eat them within a day. Reheat the rice with a splash of water under a lid until it is steaming hot all the way through. Pick the meat off any leftover thighs for wraps or salads the next day.
