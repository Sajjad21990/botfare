# Weeknight red lentil dal that freezes well

Split red lentils need no soaking and collapse into a creamy dal in about 20 minutes, which makes them the quickest pulse for a weeknight. Eat half tonight and freeze half, or double it. Freeze the plain dal and fry a fresh tadka when you reheat, so the spices taste bright instead of dull.

## Ingredients

- 250 g (1¼ cups) split red lentils
- 1 litre (4 cups) water
- ½ tsp ground turmeric
- 1 tbsp neutral oil
- 1 medium onion (about 150 g), finely chopped
- 3 garlic cloves, grated
- 2.5 cm (1 in) piece of ginger, grated
- 1 green chilli, slit lengthways (optional)
- 1 tsp ground cumin
- 1 tsp ground coriander
- 200 g chopped tomatoes (half a 400 g tin)
- 1¼ tsp fine salt, plus more to taste
- Juice of ½ lemon and a handful of chopped coriander, to finish

For the tadka:

- 2 tbsp ghee or neutral oil
- ½ tsp black mustard seeds
- 1 tsp cumin seeds
- 2 dried red chillies
- 2 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- A pinch of asafoetida (optional)

## Method

1. Rinse the lentils in a sieve under cold water until the water runs almost clear. This washes off loose starch that would otherwise foam over the pot.
2. Put the lentils, water and turmeric in a heavy saucepan and bring to the boil. Skim off the grey foam, then simmer, partly covered, for 20 to 25 minutes, until the lentils have broken down completely. Turmeric needs this cooking to lose its raw taste.
3. While the lentils cook, heat the oil in a frying pan over medium heat. Cook the onion with a pinch of salt for 8 to 10 minutes until soft and golden at the edges. Browned onion gives the dal its sweet, savoury base.
4. Add the garlic, ginger and chilli and cook for 1 minute, then stir in the ground cumin and coriander for 30 seconds. Ground spices burn quickly, so keep them moving.
5. Add the tomatoes and cook for about 5 minutes, until thick and jammy and the oil starts to separate at the edges. Cooking off their water concentrates the flavour.
6. Scrape the onion mixture into the lentils with the salt and simmer for 5 minutes more. The dal should coat a spoon thickly but still pour. Add a splash of water if it is stiff.
7. To freeze some, stop here and take out those portions before the tadka, lemon and herbs go in.
8. To serve now, make the tadka. Heat the ghee in a small pan over medium heat until it shimmers, about 180°C (355°F). Add the mustard seeds, and when they pop, after about 30 seconds, add the cumin seeds for 10 seconds. Turn the heat down a little, add the garlic and fry for 30 to 60 seconds until pale gold, then add the dried chillies for 5 to 10 seconds. Add the asafoetida, then pour everything straight over the dal. Stir in the lemon juice and coriander and taste for salt.

Serve with rice or flatbreads. The tadka is explained step by step in [tempering whole spices in hot oil](/techniques/tempering-spices).

## Freezing and reheating

Cool the plain dal quickly. Spread it in a shallow container and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking. Once it is cold, ladle it into freezer bags in one or two portions, squeeze out the air and freeze them flat. It keeps its best flavour for 3 months at -18°C (0°F).

Leave out the tadka, lemon and herbs. The tadka's aroma fades in the freezer and its fat separates on reheating, and lemon juice and fresh coriander go flat.

To reheat, run the bag under warm water for 30 seconds, tip the frozen block into a saucepan with 3 tbsp water, cover and warm over low heat for 10 to 15 minutes, stirring as it softens. The starch sets as the dal cools, so loosen each portion with 60 to 120 ml (¼ to ½ cup) of water and heat until it steams all the way through, 74°C (165°F) in the centre. Then fry a fresh tadka and add the lemon and coriander. More on cooling, containers and thawing is in [freezing soups, stews and dal](/techniques/freezing-and-reheating).
