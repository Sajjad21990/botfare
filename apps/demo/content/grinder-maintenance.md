# Grinder maintenance, step by step

A burr grinder slowly fills with old coffee. Fines pack into the chute, oils coat the burrs and go stale, and the grind you dialled in last month quietly drifts. Ten minutes once a month keeps it consistent, and you need nothing more than a brush, a vacuum and a sheet of paper.

## Why it matters

Stale grounds in the chute mix with fresh ones and add a flat, papery taste. Oil on the burrs turns rancid within a couple of weeks, and that bitterness lands in every cup. Packed fines also slow the flow through the grinder, which heats the coffee and makes the particle size less even. A clean grinder is not about looks; it is about tasting the beans you paid for.

## Before you start

Unplug the grinder. Run it empty for a few seconds first, so whatever is loose falls out, then unplug it. Lay a sheet of paper under it to catch what comes out, and take a photo of the grind setting so you can return to it.

## Step 1: open the burr chamber

Most home grinders open without tools: turn the hopper or the adjustment ring past the coarsest setting until the top burr lifts out. Keep any shims or springs together on the paper in the order they came out. If your grinder needs a screwdriver, use the right size; stripped screw heads are the most common maintenance injury a grinder suffers.

## Step 2: brush the burrs

Use a stiff, dry brush and work along the teeth, not across them. Get into the outer cutting edge, where fines pack hardest. Do not use water: wet burrs rust, and wet grounds turn into paste. A toothpick lifts what the brush misses.

## Step 3: clear the chute

The chute between the burrs and the cup is where old grounds hide. Push a pipe cleaner or a thin brush through it from both ends, then vacuum the chamber. If your grinder has a declumper or a flap at the exit, take it out and tap it clean.

## Step 4: the cleaning tablets question

Grinder cleaning tablets, made from food-safe grain, scrub oil off the burrs as you grind them through. They work, and they are useful every few months for dark-roast drinkers, whose beans are oilier. Grind a dose at a medium setting, then grind and throw away about 20 g of coffee to flush the residue out.

## Step 5: reassemble and reset zero

Put the parts back in the order you laid them out. Then find zero: with the grinder running, turn finer slowly until you just hear the burrs touch, and back off immediately. Check that this point matches your notes. If zero has moved, your burrs have shifted or worn, and your old settings will need adjusting by the same amount.

## Step 6: season and dial back in

Throw away the first 10 to 15 g you grind after cleaning; it carries dust from the chamber. Return to the setting from your photo and pull or brew one cup to check the taste. Expect to move a step or two: clean burrs often grind slightly finer than dirty ones.

## How often

- Brush the burrs and chute: monthly, or every 2 kg of coffee.
- Cleaning tablets: every three months, monthly for very dark roasts.
- Check burr wear: once a year; dull burrs make more fines and muddier cups.

A grinder that gets this routine will stay consistent for years. One that does not will slowly make every bean taste a little the same.
