# Crispy oven potatoes

Crisp roast potatoes need three things: a rough, starchy surface, fat that is already hot when the potatoes go in, and enough space and time in the oven for their water to escape. Parboiling with a little baking soda and shaking the potatoes in the pan does the first job, a preheated tray of fat does the second, and about an hour at high heat does the third. Floury potatoes give the craggiest crust. Waxy ones stay smooth and never get as crunchy.

## Ingredients

- 1.2 kg (about 2½ lb) floury potatoes, such as russets
- 2 litres (8 cups) cold water
- 1 tbsp fine salt for the water, plus flaky salt to finish
- ½ tsp baking soda (bicarbonate of soda)
- 80 ml (⅓ cup) neutral oil, duck fat or beef dripping
- A few sprigs of rosemary and 4 unpeeled garlic cloves (optional)

## Method

1. Heat the oven to 220°C (425°F), or 200°C (400°F) with the fan on. Pour the fat into a large metal roasting tray and put it in the oven for at least 10 minutes. A metal tray passes heat into the potatoes much faster than glass or ceramic.
2. Peel the potatoes and cut them into 4 to 5 cm (1½ to 2 in) chunks with plenty of flat faces and corners. Corners and edges are where the crunch forms.
3. Put the potatoes in a large pan with the cold water, salt and baking soda. Starting from cold heats the chunks evenly, so the outside does not turn to mush before the middle softens. Bring to the boil, then simmer for 8 to 10 minutes, until a knife slides easily into the edge of a piece but meets resistance in the middle. The baking soda makes the water slightly alkaline, which breaks down the outer layer of the potato faster and gives a thicker starchy coating.
4. Drain well and leave the potatoes in the colander for 2 to 3 minutes to steam dry. Wet potatoes steam in the oven instead of frying.
5. Tip them back into the empty pan, put the lid on and shake hard 4 or 5 times. The edges should look fluffy and each chunk should be coated in a thick, mashed-potato paste. That paste is what turns into the crust.
6. Take the tray out of the oven and close the door to keep the heat in. Carefully tip the potatoes into the fat. They should sizzle loudly. Turn each one to coat it and spread them out so none touch. Crowded potatoes trap steam and go soggy, so use two trays if you need to.
7. Roast for 20 minutes without opening the oven. Turn each potato with a metal spatula, scraping up any that stick, and roast for another 20 minutes. Turn them again, add the rosemary and garlic if using, and roast for 10 to 20 minutes more, until deep golden brown and crisp all over. Herbs and garlic go in late because they burn in a long roast.
8. Sprinkle with flaky salt straight away, while the surface is hot enough for it to stick, and serve at once.

## Timings and make-ahead

The whole job takes about 1 hour 15 minutes: 15 minutes to peel and parboil and 50 to 60 minutes in the oven. If your oven runs cool, give the potatoes the extra 10 minutes rather than turning up the heat, which burns the edges before the flat faces have browned.

You can parboil and rough up the potatoes up to a day ahead. Cool them and keep them uncovered on a tray in the fridge, where the surface dries out and crisps even better. They also freeze at this stage. Freeze them on a lined tray, bag them, and roast from frozen, adding 10 to 15 minutes.

## When it goes wrong

Pale, soft potatoes mean the fat was not hot enough or the tray was crowded. Potatoes that fall apart in the pan boiled too long or too hard, so check them from 7 minutes and keep the water at a gentle simmer. Potatoes that stick were turned too early, before the crust had formed and released.
