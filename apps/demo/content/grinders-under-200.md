# Choosing a home grinder under $200

Under $200 is where home grinders get interesting. Below it, compromises are everywhere; above it, you are mostly paying for convenience and refinement. In this range you can get a grinder that will genuinely improve your coffee, as long as you buy for the way you brew. We spent three weeks with three popular models and brewed filter, French press and espresso on each.

## The contenders

- **Arden K-40** ($179): an electric grinder with 40 mm conical steel burrs, 31 stepped settings and a single-dose hopper.
- **Halvard Mini 2** ($129): a hand grinder with 38 mm conical burrs, a stepless dial and a body that fits in a travel bag.
- **Okto Flatline 54** ($199): an electric grinder with 54 mm flat burrs, 60 stepped settings and a bellows to puff out leftover grounds.

## How we tested

We used one washed medium roast for the whole test. For each grinder we brewed a 15 g cone pour-over at 1:16, a 30 g French press at 1:15 and 18 g espresso shots aimed at 1:2. We sifted samples through two screens to estimate the share of fines and boulders, weighed retention after every dose, and timed how long each grinder took to process 18 g.

## Arden K-40

The K-40 is the easiest of the three to live with. You weigh beans, drop them in, press one button, and it stops when the chamber is empty. Retention averaged **0.2 g**, the best in the test, which makes it pleasant for switching coffees.

Its filter coffee was clean and sweet, and the French press was the best of the three: very few boulders at the coarse end. At the fine end, the steps are the weak point. Our espresso shots jumped from 23 s at setting 6 to 33 s at setting 5, with no way in between. You can work around this by adjusting dose by half a gram, but it is fiddly.

- **Good for:** filter and French press, people who switch beans often.
- **Weak at:** espresso fine-tuning.
- **Grind time for 18 g:** 14 s. **Noise:** moderate.

## Halvard Mini 2

The Mini 2 is the bargain and the surprise. Because the dial is stepless, it is the only grinder here that let us land espresso shots exactly where we wanted, and its consistency at the fine end was better than the cheaper electric grinders we have tried before. Retention was about **0.1 g**: you just tap it out.

The price you pay is effort. Grinding 18 g for espresso took about 50 seconds of steady cranking; 30 g for French press took nearly a minute and a half. The stepless dial also means you need to mark or note your settings, because there are no numbers to come back to.

- **Good for:** one or two cups a day, espresso on a budget, travel.
- **Weak at:** large batches, mornings when you have no patience.
- **Grind time for 18 g:** about 50 s by hand. **Noise:** almost none.

## Okto Flatline 54

The Flatline 54 produced the most even grind in our sifting tests, with the smallest share of fines at filter settings. Pour-overs drained quickly and evenly and tasted the most clear and separated of the three. Sixty steps also gave usable espresso control: adjacent settings moved shot time by about 3 seconds.

It is the least tidy machine. Retention without the bellows averaged **1.1 g**; with a few puffs it dropped to about 0.4 g. It is also loud, and the static means you will want a quick spritz of water on the beans before grinding to stop chaff from clinging to everything.

- **Good for:** pour-over lovers, people who want one machine for filter and espresso.
- **Weak at:** quiet kitchens, tidiness.
- **Grind time for 18 g:** 9 s. **Noise:** loud.

## Side by side

1. **Best for filter:** Okto Flatline 54, for its clarity. The Arden K-40 is close and easier to use.
2. **Best for espresso:** Halvard Mini 2, because stepless adjustment matters more than motor power at this price.
3. **Best all-rounder:** Okto Flatline 54, if you can live with the noise and mess.
4. **Best for convenience:** Arden K-40, with the lowest retention of the electric pair and one-button operation.

## Our pick

If you mostly brew filter or French press, buy the **Arden K-40** and do not look back. If espresso is the main event and you only make a couple of shots a day, the **Halvard Mini 2** gives you better control for less money, as long as you do not mind the workout. If you want a single electric grinder for everything and value cup clarity above convenience, the **Okto Flatline 54** is the strongest grinder here.

Whichever you choose, keep the burrs clean: brush them out every couple of weeks and run a cleaning cycle with a dedicated grinder-cleaning product monthly. A clean grinder at $129 will beat a dirty one at $199.
