# Freezing soups, stews and dal so they reheat well

Soups, stews, braises and dal are the best things to cook in bulk, because they freeze with almost no loss of texture. The work happens in three places: cooling the pot quickly, packing it so it freezes and thaws fast, and holding back the few ingredients that do not survive the freezer so you can add them fresh when you reheat.

## Cool it fast

Bacteria grow quickly between about 5 and 60°C (40 and 140°F), so move cooked food through that range quickly and get it into the fridge within 2 hours of cooking. A deep pot of stew stays warm in the middle for hours, so divide it into shallow containers no more than 5 cm (2 in) deep, or stand the pot in a sink of cold water and stir it for 10 minutes. Do not put a large hot pot straight into the freezer. It cools slowly there too, and it warms the food around it.

## Pack it flat and in portions

Freeze in the portions you will eat, one or two servings at a time. Ladle the cold food into zip-top freezer bags, press out the air and lay them flat on a tray to freeze. Once they are solid, stand them upright like files. A flat bag 2 cm (¾ in) thick thaws in a fraction of the time a tub takes. If you use rigid tubs, leave 2 cm of space at the top, because liquid expands by about a tenth as it freezes. Air causes freezer burn, the dry, pale patches that taste of nothing, so fill containers well and squeeze bags flat.

Label every bag with the dish, the date and what to add when you reheat it, such as water, lemon or a tadka.

## What freezes well, and what to hold back

Lentils, beans, chickpeas, tomato sauces, stocks, braised meat and blended vegetable soups all freeze well. A few ingredients do not:

- Potatoes in a stew turn grainy and spongy. Leave them out, or eat that batch fresh.
- Pasta and rice keep absorbing liquid and turn mushy. Cook them on the day, or freeze them separately.
- Cream, yogurt and soft cheese can split when reheated. Stir them in at the end instead.
- Fresh herbs, lemon juice and a finishing tadka lose their brightness. Add them after reheating.

Cook vegetables a little short of done in anything you plan to freeze, because reheating cooks them further. Go easy on the salt too, since you can adjust it at the end.

## How long it keeps

At -18°C (0°F), frozen food stays safe indefinitely, but its flavour and texture fade. Soups, stews and dal are at their best within 3 months.

## Thawing

The safest way is overnight in the fridge, where a flat 500 g bag thaws in 8 to 12 hours. For the same day, put the sealed bag in a bowl of cold water and change the water every 30 minutes. A flat bag thaws in about an hour. Dal, soup and stew can also go straight from frozen into the pan. Run the bag under warm water for 30 seconds, tip the block into a saucepan with 3 tbsp water, cover and heat gently, breaking it up as it softens. Do not thaw food on the counter, because the outside sits warm for hours while the middle is still frozen.

## Reheating

Reheat on the hob over low to medium heat with the lid on, stirring often, or in the microwave in short bursts, stirring between each. Heat it until it is steaming hot all the way through, 74°C (165°F) in the centre. Reheat only what you will eat, and only once.

Dal and bean stews thicken in the freezer as their starch sets, so add 60 to 120 ml (¼ to ½ cup) of water per portion as they warm and stir until smooth. Taste for salt again, then add the fresh finish: a squeeze of lemon, chopped herbs, a spoon of yogurt or a fresh tadka.
