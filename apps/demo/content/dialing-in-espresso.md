# Dialing in espresso at home

"Dialing in" is the process of finding the grind setting and recipe that make a particular coffee taste its best on your equipment. It has a reputation for wasting half a bag of beans. It does not have to, if you change one thing at a time and taste with a plan.

## Start with a recipe, not a guess

An espresso recipe has three numbers:

- **Dose**: the dry coffee in the basket, in grams.
- **Yield**: the liquid espresso in the cup, in grams.
- **Time**: from when you start the pump to when you stop it.

Write the recipe as a ratio of dose to yield. A sensible starting point for most medium roasts is **1:2**, for example 18 g in and 36 g out, in **26 to 32 seconds**. Lighter roasts often want a longer ratio (1:2.3 to 1:2.6); darker roasts often taste better shorter (1:1.6 to 1:1.8).

Use the dose your basket was designed for. A basket marked 18 g wants roughly 17.5 to 18.5 g. Overfill it and the puck touches the shower screen; underfill it and water pools on top and channels through.

## Tools that make it easier

You need a scale that reads to 0.1 g and fits under the portafilter, and a timer (most scales have one). A cheap needle distribution tool helps break up clumps before tamping. Everything else is optional.

## The method

Keep the dose fixed. Keep the target yield fixed. Use **grind size** to control time. Use **taste** to decide whether to change the ratio.

1. Pull the first shot at your starting grind and stop it at the target yield. Note the time.
2. If it ran fast (under about 24 s), grind finer by a small step. If it ran slow (over about 34 s), grind coarser.
3. Repeat until the time lands inside the window. This usually takes two or three shots.
4. Now taste. Do not adjust time any more; adjust ratio.

## Reading the taste

Once the time is in range, the shot will tell you which way to go.

- **Sour, sharp, thin, finishes quickly**: under-extracted. Increase the yield by 3 to 4 g (for example 36 g to 40 g) and grind slightly finer to keep the time in range.
- **Bitter, dry, hollow, lingering ashy finish**: over-extracted. Decrease the yield by 3 to 4 g, or grind slightly coarser.
- **Sour and bitter at once**: this is usually channelling, not recipe. Water found a weak path through the puck. Work on preparation before you touch the grinder again.
- **Sweet, balanced, a finish that fades cleanly**: stop. Write it down.

A useful trick is to taste the shot in two halves. Split the first and second half of the pour into separate cups. The first half shows you acidity and sweetness, the second shows bitterness. If the second half is unpleasant, shorten the ratio.

## Puck preparation that actually matters

Most inconsistency comes from the puck, not the grinder.

- **Distribute before tamping.** Stir the grounds with a fine needle tool to break up clumps, then level the bed with a few gentle taps.
- **Tamp level, not hard.** Firm and even beats heavy. A tilted tamp makes one side of the puck thinner, and water will find it.
- **Keep the basket dry and clean.** Wipe it between shots.
- **Lock in and start the pump promptly.** A puck sitting on a hot group head starts to cook.

## Temperature and pre-infusion

If your machine lets you set brew temperature, start around **93 °C**. Go a degree or two higher for very light roasts that taste sour even at long ratios, and a degree or two lower for dark roasts that taste burnt. If your machine has a pre-infusion setting, a 5 to 8 second low-pressure soak helps reduce channelling, especially with lighter coffees. Count pre-infusion as part of your total time.

## When to start over

Beans change as they age. A coffee that ran at 28 seconds on day 5 after roasting may run at 24 seconds on day 18, because the beans have lost gas and resist water less. Expect to grind a little finer through the life of a bag. If a new bag behaves very differently, go back to step 1 instead of chasing small adjustments.

## A worked example

A washed medium roast, 18 g dose, target 1:2.

- Shot 1: 36 g in 21 s. Sour. Grind two steps finer.
- Shot 2: 36 g in 27 s. Better, slightly sharp. Keep grind, increase yield.
- Shot 3: 40 g in 30 s. Sweet, round, clean finish. Done.

Three shots, about 54 g of coffee. Record the dose, yield, time and grind setting on the bag, and tomorrow's first shot will be close.
